## Why strangler fig

In a financial system the cost of being wrong is high, and the cost of stopping is even higher. Strangler fig lets you swap the engine while the car is moving: each new module is born next to the old one, proves it produces the same result, and only then takes over.

> COBOL wasn't the problem. The problem was not knowing, with tests, what it did.

## Tests as the contract

Before writing a single line of C#, the old program's behaviour becomes characterization tests. That turns tacit knowledge into versioned specification — and gives the team confidence to evolve the rules after the migration.

## What I take from it

- Modernization is more about **risk management** than technology.
- Ubiquitous language (DDD) dramatically shortens conversations with the business.
- Clean Architecture pays for itself the first time someone says "we need to replace the integration".
